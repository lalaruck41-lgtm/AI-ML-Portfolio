Project context

From analytical question to decision support

This artifact documents coursework in the M.S. Artificial Intelligence program. The work develops a structured approach to quantitative problem-solving: formulate the question, select an appropriate analytical method, interpret the output, and explain what the evidence can — and cannot — support.

The coursework connects predictive analytics with optimization, two complementary disciplines: forecasting estimates what may happen, while optimization evaluates choices under defined objectives and constraints.

Methods and concepts

Analytical building blocks

01 · Predictive analysis

Regression and forecasting

Use regression relationships to examine how variables relate to an outcome and to support forecasts within the limits of the available data and model assumptions.

02 · Model assessment

Interpreting model performance

Consider measures such as R² and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) to assess explanatory fit and forecast error, while recognizing that no single metric tells the whole story.

03 · Decision analysis

Optimization modeling

Frame a decision through an objective, decision variables, and constraints. A model is only useful when its assumptions reflect the real decision context.

04 · Communication

Evidence-based interpretation

Translate quantitative output into a clear explanation of implications, limitations, and potential next steps.

A practical workflow

How I approach an applied analytics problem

  1. Define the decision: identify the outcome or choice the analysis is intended to inform.
  2. Understand the data: inspect variables, quality, and relevant assumptions.
  3. Select the method: match regression, forecasting, or optimization to the question.
  4. Evaluate the output: interpret fit and error measures in context rather than relying on a single statistic.
  5. Communicate implications: explain what the analysis supports, where uncertainty remains, and what decision-makers should consider next.
Key takeaway

Good analytics is not just a model output.

It is a transparent chain of reasoning that connects the question, data, method, evaluation, and decision.

Professional relevance

Connecting quantitative methods to business strategy

In consumer insights and pricing work, organizations routinely need to forecast outcomes, evaluate trade-offs, and make decisions with incomplete information. Regression and optimization provide a foundation for approaching these questions systematically.
